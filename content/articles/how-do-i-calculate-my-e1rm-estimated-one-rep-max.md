Understanding your estimated 1-rep max (E1RM) is essential for effective strength training. The E1RM tells you the maximum weight you could theoretically lift for a single repetition, based on a set you've already performed. This guides your workout intensity, helps you set [progressive overload](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/articles/strength-training-for-beginners-understanding-key-phrases-like-1rm-pr-rpe-and-more) targets, and lets you track strength gains across different rep ranges.

## What Is an E1RM?

An [estimated one-rep max (E1RM)](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/articles/what-is-an-e1rm-estimated-one-rep-max) is a calculated prediction of the maximum weight you could lift for exactly one repetition. Rather than attempting a [dangerous maximal lift](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/articles/how-often-should-i-test-my-one-rep-max), you perform a set at a submaximal weight — say, 5 or 8 reps — and plug those numbers into a formula.

This is especially useful for:

- **Programming training percentages** — most strength programs prescribe work as a percentage of 1RM (e.g., "75% of 1RM for 5×5")
- **[Tracking strength progress](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/visualizer)** over time without frequent maximal testing
- **Comparing lifts** across different sessions and rep ranges

## The E1RM Formulas

Exercise scientists have developed multiple formulas for estimating the one-rep max. Each uses the weight lifted and the number of reps completed:

- [Brzycki Formula](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/calculator/brzycki-formula-1rm-calculator): 1RM = Weight / (1.0278 - (0.0278 × Reps))
- [Epley Formula](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/calculator/epley-formula-1rm-calculator): 1RM = Weight × (1 + (0.0333 × Reps))
- [Lombardi Formula](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/calculator/lombardi-formula-1rm-calculator): 1RM = Weight × (Reps^0.10)
- [Mayhew Formula](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/calculator/mayhew-1rm-formula-calculator): 1RM = (100 × Weight) / (52.2 + (41.9 × e^(−0.055 × Reps)))
- [O'Conner Formula](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/calculator/oconner-formula-1rm-calculator): 1RM = Weight × (1 + (0.025 × Reps))
- [Wathan Formula](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/calculator/wathan-1rm-formula-calculator): 1RM = (100 × Weight) / (48.8 + (53.8 × e^(−0.075 × Reps)))

These are documented on the [Wikipedia page for one-repetition maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-repetition_maximum).

## A Step-by-Step Example

Say you [squat](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/progress-guide/squat) 225 lb for 5 reps. Here's what each formula estimates as your 1RM:

- [Brzycki](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/calculator/brzycki-formula-1rm-calculator?weight=225&reps=5&calcIsMetric=false): 253 lb
- [Epley](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/calculator/epley-formula-1rm-calculator?weight=225&reps=5&calcIsMetric=false): 262 lb
- [Lombardi](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/calculator/lombardi-formula-1rm-calculator?weight=225&reps=5&calcIsMetric=false): 267 lb
- [Mayhew](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/calculator/mayhew-1rm-formula-calculator?weight=225&reps=5&calcIsMetric=false): 256 lb
- [O'Conner](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/calculator/oconner-formula-1rm-calculator?weight=225&reps=5&calcIsMetric=false): 253 lb
- [Wathan](https://www.strengthjourneys.xyz/calculator/wathan-1rm-formula-calculator?weight=225&reps=5&calcIsMetric=false): 258 lb

The results cluster around 253–267 lb — a useful working range for setting your training percentages.

## Which Formula Is Most Accurate?

It depends on your rep range:

- **Brzycki and Epley** are the most widely used and generally most accurate for sets in the **2–10 rep range**. Brzycki tends to be slightly more conservative.
- **Mayhew and Wathan** use exponential functions and perform better at **higher rep ranges (10–20 reps)**.
- **Lombardi** is the simplest and tends to **overestimate** slightly, especially at higher reps.
- **O'Conner** produces the most conservative estimates across all rep ranges.

As a general rule: **the closer your working set is to 1 rep, the more accurate any formula will be.** E1RM estimates become increasingly unreliable beyond 10 reps — there's simply too much variability in muscular endurance between individuals.

## How to Use Your E1RM in Training

Once you have your estimated 1RM, you can use it to prescribe percentage-based working sets:

- **90–95% of 1RM** → 1–2 rep sets (near-maximal strength)
- **80–85% of 1RM** → 3–5 rep sets (strength focus)
- **70–75% of 1RM** → 6–10 rep sets (strength and hypertrophy)
- **60–65% of 1RM** → 12–15 rep sets (hypertrophy and endurance)

Most popular strength programs — Starting Strength, 5/3/1, GZCLP — build their progression around these percentages.
