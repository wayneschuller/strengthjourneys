Updates from September 27 to October 3.

### Evergreen

Strength Journeys has a new theme, and it might be our favourite yet. Evergreen is pine, cream and brass, in a light and a dark version, with warm serif headings and its own logo: the wordmark between two leafy brass barbells.

It is a theme that grows. Leaves hang into the nav bar, a sprig sits in the corner of every card, and no two cards on a page get the same one. Turn on the animated background and the leaves sway in a light breeze over drifting contour lines. Lifting is a long game, and this is a theme for people playing it. Pick it from the palette button in the nav.

### Your Meets, Marked in Gold

Strength Journeys now recognises the days you competed. It reads them from your Label column, or from notes that name the meet or number your attempts, and it understands Australian comp phrasing too. Each one gets a gold medal on your [strength](/visualizer) and [tonnage](/tonnage) charts, the Long Game heatmaps and your streak bars, and hovering a chart names the meet.

Open a past meet day in [the log](/log) and a gold card leads the page: the meet's name, how long ago it was, the squat, bench and deadlift that counted, your note on each attempt, and your total. Filmed attempts get big play buttons, and Share turns the whole day into an image card. PRs set on the platform wear the medal wherever they appear, and the AI Coach knows your meet history too.

### A Story of the Day

The home dashboard now greets you with one line from your own training: a PR from this week, the anniversary of a meet or of the day you started lifting, a milestone within reach, this year's lifetime bests, or a classic lift from years back. It changes as you visit, says why it is showing, and links to the session or chart behind it.

The Week in Iron celebrates a three-session week with a "Week won" banner. The Month in Iron shows what is up next early in the month, and The Long Game has the most room on the page.

### The Log Knows What's Next

Open an empty day and [the log](/log) leads with the Big Four lift you are due to train, with a target one step up from last time. Newer lifters get a full suggested workout of three lifts.

Working sets that beat your recent sessions at the same reps earn a progression badge, and the badge counts your streak as it grows. Added reps count as progress too. Hover a strength badge to see your e1RM, the next strength level, and the set that gets you there.

Each lift card has a new header with bigger artwork, and the date bar jumps to your first session or back to today.

### Video Links in One Tap

Copy a video link, open the log, and the set offers to attach it. One tap and it is saved. A set's video mark opens a small menu to play, edit, copy or remove the link, and you can Tab through reps, weight, notes and video as one form.

### More AI Coaches

The [AI Lifting Assistant](/ai-lifting-assistant) adds DeepSeek V4 Flash for everyone and Claude Sonnet 5.5 with a Google sign-in. The model switcher shows whether each model's weights are open and roughly what an answer costs us to produce. If a model you picked is having a bad day, the coach answers with the default model so you still get a reply.

### Strength Potential, Redrawn

On every [lift guide](/progress-guide), strength potential is now a dumbbell chart: a dot for your best set at each rep count, joined to a ring for what your strongest set says you can do. A callout names the easiest PR to chase next, and you can click any rep count to target that one. Switch between Lifetime and 12 months to project from your recent strength.

[![Back squat strength potential by rep range, with an easiest PR callout](/changelog/2026-10-03/image-4.webp "The gap between your best set and your potential, rep by rep")](/progress-guide/squat)

### Plate Milestones

[Plate milestones](/plate-milestones) load the bar as you earn each tier, with tier marks on the slider and a dot on each sparkline for the day you first crossed a plate.

### More Themes

Evergreen is not the only one with fresh paint. Starry Night is repainted as a Van Gogh canvas with a brushstroke nav, Retro Arcade has neon glow, sunset buttons and scoreboard numbers, and Blueprint has drafting-style buttons over a faint grid.

### Finding Your Way Around

- The Big Four menu is now a Lifts menu: the Big Four, then your ten most trained lifts, then the [Lift Explorer](/lift-explorer).
- New and Updated pills mark the tools that changed this month.
- Hover What's New in the nav for a preview of the latest entry.
- The [calculator](/calculator) has a full guide to the e1RM formulas below the tool.
- Articles link to each other and to the tools and lift guides they mention.
- The demo lifter is now a twelve-week novice, so you can explore every page with a believable log before linking your own.

### On Your Phone

- The log has bigger lift artwork and a full-width custom set button.
- Chart cards keep their controls on one row, and the AI actions open clear of the next card.
- The strength potential chart uses the full width, and a tap picks a rep count.

### Under the Hood

- If your connection drops, your sheet stays linked and a notice retries for you.
- Mixed kg and lb logs compare PRs by real load everywhere.
- The short-range strength charts mark every session with a dot.

Keep lifting!
