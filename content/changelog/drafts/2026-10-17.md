---
title: "A log that works offline, and an import that tells your story"
---

Updates from October 4 to October 17.

### Log Without a Connection

Gyms are famous for bad reception, so [the log](/log) now works without one. Your phone keeps a copy of your sheet, and the log opens straight onto your own sessions whether you have signal or not. Add, edit and delete sets as usual, and they go to your sheet as soon as the connection comes back.

Close the tab before that happens and your sets are still safe. The next time you open Strength Journeys, it picks up where you left off and sends them through.

Patchy mobile data is handled better too. A dropped request no longer looks like a sign-out, so you stay in your own log instead of landing in the demo.

### Your Import, Told as a Story

The [import page](/import) now opens with the whole picture of your training: years under the bar, weight moved, meets, plate milestones, your longest streak, your biggest year and your favourite day to train, right beside your strength ranking and 1000lb Club total. The headline numbers open your home dashboard, and the strength card opens your full ranking.

It also asks about you in a sentence you can correct: "I am a 30 year old man and I weigh 185 lb." We start from a guess based on your squat, bench and deadlift, and nothing is saved until you confirm it. Units follow the file you bring, so a kilogram lifter's import stays in kilograms.

### More Files Come Through

- Strong exports from the app in French now import, and Spanish headings are read too. Warm-up, drop and failure sets come across with a note saying so.
- When a Strong file does not say its unit, your own unit setting decides.
- Dips, push ups, inverted rows, pistol squats and other bodyweight movements import as lifts of their own, with an e1RM that counts the share of bodyweight each one moves.
- A spreadsheet you kept in your own words can be imported, even with headings like "Day" or "Count", and "Weight (kg)" over a column means kilograms.
- When an import leaves something out, the notice names the exercises and says why in plain words.
- TurnKey imports read in the unit most of the file uses, and recognise a coach's scheduled workout that you logged a day or two either side.
- If a merge would add sets for a lift your sheet already has on that day, it checks with you first.

### Six New Variation Guides

The [box squat](/progress-guide/box-squat), [safety bar squat](/progress-guide/safety-bar-squat), [incline bench press](/progress-guide/incline-bench-press), [sumo deadlift](/progress-guide/sumo-deadlift), [deficit deadlift](/progress-guide/deficit-deadlift) and [push press](/progress-guide/push-press) each have a full guide now, and the [push jerk](/progress-guide/push-jerk) joins the lift registry with a guide of its own.

Every Big Four guide shows its variations as tiles, and each variation links back to its parent and its siblings. Guides also link across to the lifts that go with them, so one guide leads naturally to the next.

### Your Sheet, Your Layout

Strength Journeys has always read your sheet by its headings, wherever your columns sit. Now it writes that way too. Sets from the log and rows from an import land under the right headings in whatever order you keep them, and a column of your own is left alone.

- If your sheet is missing a heading, Strength Journeys puts it back for you and tells you what it added.
- A sheet caught mid-edit stays linked, and comes back on its own once it reads cleanly again.
- Fixing a mistyped date works on any layout.

### Any Colour You Like

The lift colour slider is now a full colour picker with a hex field, so you can give each lift exactly the shade you want and watch the charts follow along.

### Under the Hood

- Pages are lighter and load faster, and the Google tag waits until the browser is idle.
- Icon buttons are named for screen readers, and every corner follows your theme.
- Deleting a session keeps you on the same day.
- Starting a lift from a link adds it to today only.
- The landing page has fresh testimonials from lifters who wrote in.

Keep lifting!
